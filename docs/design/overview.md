# Game Overview

Isekai-style online co-op action RPG. Players die in their old life, are reincarnated into a new world with nothing, and survive, grow stronger and earn fame together as a party.

## Premise
- The game opens in an extremely dangerous situation. The player can walk around freely within it, but it is not survivable. Eventually something kills the player and everything goes dark.
- The player then takes the form of an astral body in a scene surrounded by the cosmos. A voice explains, through dialogue, what happened to them and what happens next: reincarnation.
- In the cosmic space the player:
  - creates the visual appearance of their new character
  - chooses a character class
  - chooses a single item
- Something then thrusts them into the new world, where they wake up with nothing.

## What makes the player special
- The status system is innate to the new world. Natives have one too, but it does not manifest visually for them.
- Otherworlders (the players) see it as a status window, which shows their stats and information about their new life.

## Core gameplay loop
The game revolves around **exploration, advancement and survival**. Players must survive against monsters, get stronger, and make money in order to keep surviving.

Ways to make money:
- items dropped by monsters
- rare materials and artifacts found in various places
- quests from the guild and from citizens in populated areas

## Classes
- Magic Swordsman, Archer, Assassin, Tank. More classes may come later.
- A secret class, the Predator, can only use abilities it steals from monsters. For now it is available only to the developer.

## Combat
- Real-time. It should feel intuitive and almost addicting: satisfying but difficult.
- Genuine skill checks mixed in, such as parrying, weak spots and stealth.
- Each character class starts with a set of extremely basic default combat abilities.
- Players develop new abilities or combat mechanics through combat or by acquiring them as they progress.
- A stamina bar limits sprinting, dodge rolls and heavy attacks, and refills after a short pause.

## Progression
- Killing monsters gives experience, and experience raises the player's level.
- New skills come from:
  - spell books or skill books, recovered from monsters with luck, or bought
  - a skill tree within the player window, which advances with experience points
- Candidate stats: attack, defense, stealth, luck, dexterity, mana, stamina. These may vary by character type.
- Each level gives stat points that the player allocates in the status window.

## Items
- The inventory is a slot list (about 20 slots). Items stack; there is no weight limit. It is shown in the status window.

## Co-op
- Up to 4 players, who can form one party.
- Party members' abilities synergize based on character type. Example: a support character buffs a swordsman's movement speed or strength.
- Parties take on dungeons and build fame. This is what the game is really about.
- Solo play is possible but less complex and less enjoyable.
- Parties are not formed automatically. Players must find each other. Two players can form a party of 2, and the others can join later.
- Joining a party requires some kind of initiation or pact.
- In co-op, players must be in a party to enter the capital, because an individual is far too suspicious.

## World
- Small towns, forests, and the capital (the main city). Mountains possibly.
- The map stays relatively small and well optimized, but exploration should still matter.
- Many kinds of dungeons, randomly generated so each one is unique, built in a way that keeps the game enjoyable.
- Fame means guild advancement, such as guild rank. Higher rank unlocks new opportunities.

## Tone
Mostly light, with some moments of darkness. Kept modest, given this is not a AAA game.

## Art style
- Cartoon style, slightly less than Pixar style.
- Medium-low polycount: not high, and not the lowest possible, at least for characters.
- Vibrant colors. Glowing elements will likely play a part.
- Everything should run efficiently without lacking detail.

## First 30 minutes
1. The player wakes up in a semi-random location.
2. They look around in a short cinematic, then are made to look up as the status window pops up with a brief rundown.
3. From then on, the status window pops up whenever a crucial piece of information is needed, like a tutorial.
4. The player explores and inevitably encounters enemies and items such as food, materials or magic items.
5. In co-op, the status window advises players to find their party members. Players who find each other can form a party.
6. After 30 minutes, players may or may not be in a party, and may or may not have reached a town.

## Still undecided
- The deadly opening scenario, and what kills the player.
- What thrusts the player from the cosmic space into the new world.
- Whether the class and single-item choice in the cosmic space stays (may change).
- The final stat list, and how stats differ by class.
- Combat mechanics in depth (parrying, weak spots, stealth).
- Parties of AI native adventurers for solo players.
- Larger sessions with multiple parties.
- Whether procedural generation is feasible for the world, and whether mountains are included.
- How dungeons are randomly generated, and which kinds exist.
- Guild ranks, and what each one unlocks.
- The pact used to join a party (for example, a blood pact).
- Whether solo players also need a party to enter the capital.
- What "information about their new life" the status window shows.
- How much darkness and real emotion the tone includes.
- Who lives in the world besides natives, guild members and citizens.
